## 2. How combinatorial data validate single-KO biomarker predictions

Logic: if DepMap shows "loss/low expression of paralog A -> dependency on paralog B", a double-KO screen should show A+B KO sub-additive (negative GI) in lines where A is expressed, while the B single KO is mild. Conversely, a biomarker-defined dependency on B in A-null lines is validated by showing B KO hurts A-null lines more than A-proficient lines (e.g., Ito: DUSP4/DUSP6 [search]).

Reported concordance (all from De Kegel preprint unless noted) [verified]:
- Per-screen hit rate 6-15% (median ~11%).
- 68-86% of hits in the Thompson, Parrish and CHyMErA screens are cell-line specific; Dede is the exception (~79% of hits in >=2 lines, but only 3 lines).
- Mean pairwise hit overlap between screens ~31% (range ~19% Thompson-Dede to ~44% Thompson-Parrish). In RPE-1, screened by both Thompson and CHyMErA, only 2 of 26 hits (~8%) overlapped.
- Screened pairs are biased: higher sequence identity, smaller families (median 3 vs 11), 73% vs 19% whole-genome duplicates, 89% vs 13% closest paralogs.
- A classifier using features (including DepMap-derived molecular data) reached average precision ~49% on the 4-screen consensus (50 SL vs 407 non-SL) vs ~20% for sequence identity alone; chance ~11%.
- 2026 preprint: inter-screen reproducibility estimated at precision 0.40, recall 0.32; context-specific model ROC AUC 0.87 (seen pairs) / 0.81 (unseen pairs) on 20 unseen Klingbeil lines [verified].
- Dede 2020: 24 SL pairs missed by monogenic screens (paralog masking), via DepMap-mined candidates [verified].
- Esmaeili Anvar 2024: other platforms show high sensitivity in one screen but variable across screens; Cas12a gave the best within-platform replicability; Inzolia sensitivity ~75% on candidate gold standards (9/12) [verified/search].

Implications: (i) combinatorial data validate at best ~30-50% of paralog predictions; a failure to replicate is weak evidence against a prediction, and a success is strong evidence. (ii) Use "hit in >=2 screens or >=2 lines" as the gold standard rather than any single screen (Esmaeili Anvar construction, score > 0.25 and >=2 studies). (iii) Combinatorial screens only cover paralog/known-interaction pairs, so they can validate only the paralog-buffering subset of our biomarker-dependency associations; they say nothing about mutation- or lineage-type biomarkers.

## 4. Design implications (recommendations)

1. Use Chronos CN-corrected gene effects from a single DepMap release; log the release version and screen library; do not mix Chronos with older CERES/RNAi values.
2. Fit, for every gene-biomarker test, a linear model: gene effect ~ biomarker + lineage + medium/growth pattern + screen library + TMB/MSI + TP53 + proliferation proxy + gene-level CN. Report the biomarker coefficient and the change in it after adding each covariate block (a "confounding delta").
3. Make lineage handling dual: pooled model with lineage covariate, plus within-lineage tests combined by meta-analysis. Require direction consistency in at least two lineages (or flag as single-lineage).
4. Culture-medium audit: tabulate medium and growth pattern against lineage; where they nest (heme/suspension) treat as unidentifiable and test only within a stratum. Inspect the current release for actual column names before coding; I could not confirm them.
5. Co-location filter: for each biomarker hit, enumerate all genes within the same CN segment/chromosome arm, compute call or CN correlation (e.g., >0.8), run conditional models (biomarker | neighbour), and collapse tightly co-altered genes into one locus-level biomarker. Always check the known examples CDKN2A/MTAP/9p21 and ENO1/1p36 as positive controls for this procedure.
6. Collateral-lethality class: label associations where the biomarker is a probable passenger and the dependency is a paralog (e.g., ENO1->ENO2, MTAP->PRMT5) as a distinct category, not as a driver subtype.
7. Multiple testing: BH FDR within biomarker families, hierarchical (biomarker-level then gene-level) control, and a lineage-stratified permutation null as the primary calibration; report Storey q as sensitivity. Pre-register the biomarker list size.
8. Burden covariates: include TMB and MSI as covariates and run a hypermutator-excluded sensitivity analysis; prefer hotspot/predicted-damaging mutation calls over any-coding.
9. Combinatorial validation tier: for predictions of the form "loss of A -> dependency on paralog B", look them up in Klingbeil 2024, Parrish 2021, Dede 2020, Thompson 2021, Ito 2021 and Esmaeili Anvar 2024 tables. Label each prediction replicated (hit in >=1 independent screen, ideally >=2 lines), tested-not-replicated, or untested. Do not penalise tested-not-replicated heavily: expected inter-screen precision ~0.40 and recall ~0.32.
10. Selection-bias caveat: screened pairs are enriched for close, whole-genome-duplicate paralogs, so report validation rates against a matched background, not against all Ensembl paralogs.
11. Match cell contexts: map the screened lines (A375, A549, HT29, OVCAR8, PC9, HeLa, K562, NCI-H1299, MDA-MB-231, etc.) to DepMap IDs, and test the biomarker prediction in the same line's DepMap profile; compare prediction in screen lines vs overall to avoid line-specific artefacts.
12. Data engineering: script downloads for GEO GSE178179 (Parrish), figshare (Dede, Esmaeili Anvar, 2026 predictions), and bioRxiv supplementary tables (Klingbeil); mark Thompson raw data (EGA) and any unverified source (CHyMErA, Ito, Horlbeck, Najm) as manual, and check licences (Parrish article is CC BY-NC-ND; others CC BY 4.0) before redistributing derived tables.
